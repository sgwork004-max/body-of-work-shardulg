History Lit Fest

Three weeks of ground-level outreach to bring cafés and colleges on board as promotion partners for a history literature festival. High-volume cold pitching, real-time negotiation, and a tracking sheet that turned rejection into strategy. The mindset shift that made it work: we're not asking for space – we're offering value. The event went on to draw 5,000+ attendees.

01 · the system

Map the city, pitch by neighbourhood.

Mapped cafés by locality and worked neighbourhood-first – Kothrud, Shivaji Nagar, Viman Nagar – with a pitch structure, talking points, and a feedback capture sheet from day one. Approvals were tracked in real time; rejection patterns fed straight back into the next day's pitch. The loop was the system:

map area→pitch→log response→find the pattern→iterate

02 · the floor

What one hundred and thirteen pitches teach.

on selling

Energy sets the tone

Open posture, steady eye contact, a personal anecdote. In high-volume live pitching these are not soft skills; they are the mechanism.

on gatekeepers

The manager decides first

Managers, waiters, and assistants decide whether you ever reach the owner. Treat them as the first pitch, because they are.

on confidence

The 30th pitch

Somewhere around pitch thirty, the fear of rejection stopped arriving. What replaced it was ownership of the story being told.

on influence

The HOD who asked back

A college department head heard the pitch – then asked for help with his own outreach project. When the audience asks for the playbook, the playbook works.

moments that mattered

What mattered.

  • first yesA skeptical diner owner said yes – and threw in a discount. The first crack in the wall.
  • the 26-café dayBack-to-back pitches across the city, ending with plane spotting in Viman Nagar. Exhausting and brilliant in equal measure.
  • the panelPresented the model to senior faculty and mentors; recognised for building an original, scalable outreach system.

field note

What changed my judgment.

Quantity cannot replace real conversations – the most successful pitches were the most human ones. A tight master sheet finds patterns a tired brain cannot. And resourcefulness beats polish whenever the timeline is short. The cold-outreach muscle built here carried directly into every BD role that followed.
