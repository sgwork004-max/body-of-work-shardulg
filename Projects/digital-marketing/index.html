Digital Marketing

A two-week sprint from a barely-alive concept to a live, paying event – with no budget and a fragile team. A full funnel built on free tools, manual lead collection, cold calls to strangers, and last-minute urgency offers. At 10 AM on event day, two people had paid. By close, fifteen. Two at ten is not a verdict; it's data – treating it as a verdict would have made it one.

01 · the funnel

Top to bottom on free tools.

Studied funnel mechanics and then built one with no money: on-ground lead collection in Koregaon Park at the top, cold messages and calls through the middle, and urgency offers at the bottom – a Spotify Premium deal closed the late-stage holdouts. Email automation ran on Brevo triggered by Google Forms, discovered late at night because nobody had the answer and the budget was zero.

tofu · leads on foot→mofu · 100+ messages→bofu · urgency offer→ticket sold

02 · event day

The morning of two.

The venue was 1,300 square feet, sold and decorated over weeks. At 10 AM, two people had paid. The gut punch was real; so was the finding underneath it: event conversion is extremely back-loaded. Most people decide at the last minute, and urgency is only real when the deadline is. The final hour of selling was worth more than the previous ten days combined. The event closed at fifteen attendees, in a venue set up from scratch in four hours on ₹1,000.

03 · the team

The sentence nobody wrote.

Mid-project, the team quietly split over who was still working on it. Nobody said "I'm out" – people just stopped showing up, and one messy group-chat message spiralled into a fight that nearly killed the project. The durable fix, applied to every project since: roles and ownership get written down and confirmed before work starts. Verbal alignment dissolves; written alignment persists.

field note

What changed my judgment.

Clarity beats cleverness – the simple messages converted. Urgency moves people more than features do. Free tools go much further than most people ever test. And a two-person morning is survivable if you keep selling until the doors open.
