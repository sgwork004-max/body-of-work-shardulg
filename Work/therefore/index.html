Therefore Design

Nine weeks building the business-development function of a strategy and design consultancy from the ground up: a credentials deck, a CRM recommendation, an investor outreach strategy, and 200+ direct reach-outs. The honest shape of the work is an arc – uncertainty, then execution, then recognition – and the first week belongs in it as much as the last.

01 · outputs

What left the building.

deck

The credentials deck

Multi-client case studies built story-first and iterated five-plus times before final approval. The craft lesson that survived: one core message per slide, written as a sentence before the slide exists. If the slide can't be summarised in that sentence, the slide isn't ready.

system

The CRM proposal

Evaluated three platforms against the firm's lead-management needs, mapped requirements, and presented a structured recommendation to the studio lead – who accepted it. The first time advice, not just effort, was taken seriously.

strategy

Investor outreach

A target list of twenty VCs and angel investors, outreach templates, and an A/B testing framework – then the execution: seventy-eight investor emails sent in a single day in week seven.

playbook

The handover folder

Research template, targeting criteria, and messaging framework assembled into a repeatable playbook – still in use at the studio after the apprenticeship ended. Systems outlast apprentices. That was the goal.

the arc

Uncertainty to recognition, week by week.

  • weeks 1–2The rough start. Struggled to approach seniors, missed a deadline, underestimated 9.5-hour days. Kept visible because the fix – ask fast, ask specific – came from here.
  • weeks 3–4Five case studies built; the credentials deck begins iterating; CRM platforms under evaluation.
  • week 5CRM proposal presented and accepted. The turn of the arc.
  • weeks 6–7First cold DM and cold email sent; investor deck built; 78 investor emails in one day; work publicly appreciated in the BD meet.
  • weeks 8–9Deck finalised, 200+ reach-outs crossed, prospect research shared with the team, final presentation and handover delivered.

field note

What changed my judgment.

How to build a sales system from zero: ICP, prospect list, outreach, follow-up rhythm. What makes a credentials deck actually work. How to present a recommendation to leadership and have it accepted. And the one that transfers everywhere: fear of bothering people is not politeness – it's a tax the other person pays in worse output.
